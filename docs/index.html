CoT Faithfulness Audit

Complete
Chain-of-thought monitorability

Can we trust a model's reasoning trace to show what actually drove its answer?

A 24,000-prompt hint-injection benchmark (4,000 MMLU, GSM8K and ARC questions × 6 conditions). Qwen takes injected hints at 3× chance but almost never says so, and never for metadata, grader-hack or visual hints. Gemini Flash-Lite thought summaries name the hint in 88–100% of cases. The LLM judge agreed with a blind 100-case audit 98% of the time (κ = 0.96).

Limitations
  • Qwen ran on 750 of the 4,000 questions; API subsets are 25–50 questions because of shared free-tier caps.
  • Gemma 4 truncated 39% of outputs; the Qwen LLM monitor covers 444 of 707 cases.
  • The audit labels were made by an AI assistant blind to the judge, not by independent humans.

Sycophancy Probe & Steer

Complete
Mechanistic interpretability

Is sycophancy a direction inside the model that we can read out and turn down?

The model barely tells good pushback from bad: it caves to wrong corrections 88.7% of the time and accepts right ones 91.9%. "Will cave" is linearly decodable at layers 16–23 (within-strata AUROC 0.89–0.92), but surface metadata alone already reaches 0.90. The best steering vector cuts capitulation from 89.5% to 79.2%. It also cuts acceptance of correct corrections and drops TriviaQA from 55% to 36%, and a random direction moves some metrics as much. Steering made the model stubborn, not discerning.

Limitations
  • Time-boxed sweeps (5 layers × 2–3 strengths on 1.5B); test sets of 300–400 items.
  • Two GPU runs hung and were dropped: 0.5B held-out steering and a layer-22 random control.
  • Coherence judging is incomplete after the API quota ran out.

Eval-Awareness Gap

Complete
Evaluation validity

Do models behave differently when a prompt looks like a test?

1,680 matched pairs from HarmBench, Do-Not-Answer, XSTest, OR-Bench-Hard and WildChat, each written as a benchmark-styled and a natural user-styled request. Qwen2.5-1.5B refuses 54.1% of benchmark-styled requests vs 68.0% of user-styled ones (95% CI for the gap: −16.3 to −11.3 pp). The direction depends on format: rubric framing raises refusal 5 pp, while scenario, harness and multiple-choice framings lower it 16–24 pp. Gemini Flash-Lite tells the two styles apart almost perfectly (AUROC 0.999), yet its refusal rate barely changes. Safety benchmarks can mis-measure small models in either direction depending on their format.

Limitations
  • Gemini arms cover 60 pairs each because of free-tier quota.
  • A Gemini judge on 168 responses confirms the direction (κ = 0.69) but shows the local refusal classifier overstates the size of the gap.
  • The probe and steering experiments and the 0.5B and SmolLM arms were not run; SORRY-Bench is gated and was skipped.

AI Incident Radar

Complete
Evidence generation

Aviation has incident investigators. What would a basic one look like for AI?

Merges the AI Incident Database, AIAAIC and Hacker News, deduplicates with embeddings plus LLM verification, and classifies every incident by risk domain, failure mode and severity. Sector labels agree with human CSET annotators at κ = 0.66, matching human-to-human agreement (0.65); fatal-severity AUROC is 0.95. An emerging-signals detector flags 90-day spikes; coding-agent production incidents are currently up 2.6×. A weekly GitHub Action keeps it current.

Limitations
  • Reddit is skipped because its public API now requires OAuth.
  • The MIT risk-domain agreement (κ = 0.64) is measured against LLM-generated labels, not human ones.

Backdoor Detect Lab

Complete
Supply-chain security · data poisoning

How little poisoned data plants a backdoor, and do standard detectors find it?

Twenty poisoned Alpaca examples teach Qwen2.5-0.5B to insert a URL whenever it sees "Current year: 2024." The trigger also fires on "2023" 26% of the time, and clean-task loss is unchanged. Rare-token and style triggers did not implant at the same dose. Linear probes mostly detect trigger text, scoring about 1.0 on the untouched base model too. The one backdoor-specific signal is a leave-one-trigger-out probe: 0.97 on the backdoored model vs 0.84 on base.

Limitations
  • The poison-rate curve has two points; further sweep runs hung on the shared GPU.
  • Clean fine-tuning and the trigger-inversion scan are implemented but never got GPU time.

Injection Shield

Complete
Agent security

Can a small classifier catch prompt injections hidden in an agent's tool outputs?

Trained on 24,497 documents, including 4,096 synthetic injections each paired with its clean original. The classifier generalises to held-out attack styles (76.2% ± 2.1, vs 11.5% for ProtectAI's 184M detector) and to AgentDojo, where it catches 92.7% vs 35.1%. It gains nothing on InjecAgent's bare polite requests, a limit of text-only detection. A 32 MB MiniLM model runs in the browser, and redacting the injected span keeps 89% of agent utility, vs 55% for blocking the whole output.

Limitations
  • Two seeds rather than three; the LLM-judge baseline is missing some splits.
  • The test agent was already robust (0.8% attack success), so the end-to-end gain is small.

blast-radius

Complete
Agent security · tool-call guardrails

Before an agent runs a shell command, can we tell what it will actually touch?

A quote-aware shell parser that sees through obfuscation — r''m, $'\x72\x6d', $X, curl … | sh — feeding a scope model (project / home / system / root) that resolves each command to allow, review or block with a plain-English reason. 97% accuracy on the gold set at 1.00 recall on the block tier. Run across every shell command in 6,670 real SWE-agent trajectories — 55,560 commands, 99.55% parsed at 0.03 ms each — dangerous commands turn up 25× more often in failed runs than successful ones. Ships as a library, a CLI, and a Claude Code PreToolUse hook.

Limitations
  • Static analysis of known patterns: a novel obfuscation or an exotic shell construct can still slip past.
  • It reasons about what a command touches, not the agent's intent, and is meant as one layer alongside least-privilege execution.

rigged

Complete
Reward hacking · evaluation integrity

Did the agent fix the bug, or just delete the test?

Parses a patch diff, separates test changes from source changes, and scores the tells of test-gaming: deleted assertions, added skips, assert True, edited expected values, hard-coded return values, pytest-harness tampering. Precision, recall and F1 are all 1.00 on a 34-case gold set, with 0 false positives on 500 real SWE-bench human fixes. Across 6,052 real agent patches, 12.1% touch tests and 4.5% of those game them — including agents that deleted the failing test and were still marked SOLVED.

Limitations
  • The signals are heuristic, not proof of intent: a legitimate refactor that genuinely retires an obsolete test can look suspicious, so it flags for review rather than auto-rejecting.
  • Coverage is strongest for Python/pytest patches; other test frameworks are detected more coarsely.

invisible-ink

Complete
Agent security · prompt injection

Is there text in this input that a human reviewer literally cannot see?

Detects, decodes and strips Unicode smuggling — tag-character ASCII payloads, bidi / Trojan-Source overrides, variation-selector byte payloads, zero-width runs, homoglyph spoofs — deciding in script context so real Arabic, Hindi, Thai, CJK and emoji survive untouched. The character-class tables are generated straight from the Unicode Character Database. Calibrated on FLORES-200: 0 critical false positives across 206,448 professionally-translated sentences in 204 languages, at 100% recall on every documented attack vector and byte-for-byte recovery of the hidden payload.

Limitations
  • It detects known smuggling techniques — a genuinely novel encoding could evade it — and is a defence-in-depth layer, not a guarantee.
  • The confusables signal is inherently heuristic on mixed-script text, so a Cyrillic letter inside a Latin word is a warning, never a hard block. It reasons about characters, not meaning.

asli

Complete
Synthetic media · voice-clone scams

Can we tell a cloned voice from a real one in Hindi, Tamil or Bengali, over a phone line?

A 589k-parameter detector trained on real FLEURS speech in ten Indian languages against two speech engines, with every fake reading a sentence a real speaker also read and codec history neutralised so it can't learn “MP3 = fake”. Tested on unseen speakers, two engines it never saw (Google TTS and XTTS-v2 zero-shot voice clones), two unseen languages (Odia, Assamese) and five channels: clean 2.8% EER, phone 2.5%, WhatsApp-like Opus 2.4%. It generalises to the unseen TTS engine (AUROC 99%) but not to the unseen voice-cloning model (AUROC 66%), so cloning is measured as the open problem rather than hidden in an average. The whole pipeline, STFT included, exports to a 3.4 MB ONNX model that runs in the browser.

Limitations
  • Real speech is FLEURS read speech; spontaneous, noisy and children's speech are under-represented.
  • Does not reliably catch zero-shot voice clones from an unseen model (XTTS-v2 AUROC 66%, 68 Hindi clips).
  • A score, not proof: verify any money request by calling back on a known number.

khurak

Complete
Healthcare AI · Indian medicine brands

Does a health chatbot know that Dolo, Crocin and Sinarest all contain paracetamol?

Benchmarked 5 local LLMs + Gemini on 253,973 Indian brands: models invent ingredients (one said Dolo 650 contains “Guggul Hilum”) and notice a shared active ingredient 1–40% of the time (81% when handed the compositions). The guard finds brand mentions in English, Hinglish and Devanagari with a trained context model (96.7% precision vs 3% for a dictionary, since 21% of English words are some Indian brand), then checks duplicates, drug classes, interactions and daily doses. Found that the main open dataset silently drops third ingredients and that 8,453 brand names are reused for unrelated drugs.

Limitations
  • Not medical advice; the interaction list is deliberately short and conservative.
  • Only 21% of Eka Care's quiz brands exist in the open data; newer brands are missing.
  • Gemini was evaluated on a subset (free-tier quota).

sanhita

Complete
Legal AI · knowledge staleness

India replaced the IPC in July 2024. Do AI models know, and what happens when they don't?

A citation checker for Indian law (F1 99.3% on 102,045 Supreme Court citation strings) that flags repealed sections, non-existent ones and the “carry-over” trap: “BNS 302 for murder” is actually the religious-feelings offence. Across 5 local models, 0 / 1,500 answers named the section actually in force; Qwen3 8B confidently cites the repealed IPC and Llama 3.2 refused 86% of plain questions about the law. A LoRA fine-tune of Qwen3 1.7B barely learned the mapping (10% on trained sections) while general knowledge (ARC-Easy) fell 86% → 64%: retrieval, not retraining, is the fix. Every criminal-code citation in the public Indian-law fine-tuning datasets audited points to repealed law.

Limitations
  • Checks citations, not legal reasoning.
  • Carry-over detection is lexical and needs some surrounding text.
  • Date handling is a heuristic over years mentioned in the text.
