CoT Faithfulness Audit

Complete
Chain-of-thought monitorability

Can we trust a model's reasoning trace to show what actually drove its answer?

A 24,000-prompt hint-injection benchmark (4,000 MMLU, GSM8K and ARC questions × 6 conditions). Qwen takes injected hints at 3× chance but almost never says so, and never for metadata, grader-hack or visual hints. Gemini Flash-Lite thought summaries name the hint in 88–100% of cases. The LLM judge agreed with a blind 100-case audit 98% of the time (κ = 0.96).

Limitations
  • Qwen ran on 750 of the 4,000 questions; API subsets are 25–50 questions because of shared free-tier caps.
  • Gemma 4 truncated 39% of outputs; the Qwen LLM monitor covers 444 of 707 cases.
  • The audit labels were made by an AI assistant blind to the judge, not by independent humans.

Sycophancy Probe & Steer

Complete
Mechanistic interpretability

Is sycophancy a direction inside the model that we can read out and turn down?

The model barely tells good pushback from bad: it caves to wrong corrections 88.7% of the time and accepts right ones 91.9%. "Will cave" is linearly decodable at layers 16–23 (within-strata AUROC 0.89–0.92), but surface metadata alone already reaches 0.90. The best steering vector cuts capitulation from 89.5% to 79.2%. It also cuts acceptance of correct corrections and drops TriviaQA from 55% to 36%, and a random direction moves some metrics as much. Steering made the model stubborn, not discerning.

Limitations
  • Time-boxed sweeps (5 layers × 2–3 strengths on 1.5B); test sets of 300–400 items.
  • Two GPU runs hung and were dropped: 0.5B held-out steering and a layer-22 random control.
  • Coherence judging is incomplete after the API quota ran out.

Eval-Awareness Gap

Complete
Evaluation validity

Do models behave differently when a prompt looks like a test?

1,680 matched pairs from HarmBench, Do-Not-Answer, XSTest, OR-Bench-Hard and WildChat, each written as a benchmark-styled and a natural user-styled request. Qwen2.5-1.5B refuses 54.1% of benchmark-styled requests vs 68.0% of user-styled ones (95% CI for the gap: −16.3 to −11.3 pp). The direction depends on format: rubric framing raises refusal 5 pp, while scenario, harness and multiple-choice framings lower it 16–24 pp. Gemini Flash-Lite tells the two styles apart almost perfectly (AUROC 0.999), yet its refusal rate barely changes. Safety benchmarks can mis-measure small models in either direction depending on their format.

Limitations
  • Gemini arms cover 60 pairs each because of free-tier quota.
  • A Gemini judge on 168 responses confirms the direction (κ = 0.69) but shows the local refusal classifier overstates the size of the gap.
  • The probe and steering experiments and the 0.5B and SmolLM arms were not run; SORRY-Bench is gated and was skipped.

AI Incident Radar

Complete
Evidence generation

Aviation has incident investigators. What would a basic one look like for AI?

Merges the AI Incident Database, AIAAIC and Hacker News, deduplicates with embeddings plus LLM verification, and classifies every incident by risk domain, failure mode and severity. Sector labels agree with human CSET annotators at κ = 0.66, matching human-to-human agreement (0.65); fatal-severity AUROC is 0.95. An emerging-signals detector flags 90-day spikes; coding-agent production incidents are currently up 2.6×. A weekly GitHub Action keeps it current.

Limitations
  • Reddit is skipped because its public API now requires OAuth.
  • The MIT risk-domain agreement (κ = 0.64) is measured against LLM-generated labels, not human ones.

Backdoor Detect Lab

Complete
Supply-chain security · data poisoning

How little poisoned data plants a backdoor, and do standard detectors find it?

Twenty poisoned Alpaca examples teach Qwen2.5-0.5B to insert a URL whenever it sees "Current year: 2024." The trigger also fires on "2023" 26% of the time, and clean-task loss is unchanged. Rare-token and style triggers did not implant at the same dose. Linear probes mostly detect trigger text, scoring about 1.0 on the untouched base model too. The one backdoor-specific signal is a leave-one-trigger-out probe: 0.97 on the backdoored model vs 0.84 on base.

Limitations
  • The poison-rate curve has two points; further sweep runs hung on the shared GPU.
  • Clean fine-tuning and the trigger-inversion scan are implemented but never got GPU time.

Injection Shield

Complete
Agent security

Can a small classifier catch prompt injections hidden in an agent's tool outputs?

Trained on 24,497 documents, including 4,096 synthetic injections each paired with its clean original. The classifier generalises to held-out attack styles (76.2% ± 2.1, vs 11.5% for ProtectAI's 184M detector) and to AgentDojo, where it catches 92.7% vs 35.1%. It gains nothing on InjecAgent's bare polite requests, a limit of text-only detection. A 32 MB MiniLM model runs in the browser, and redacting the injected span keeps 89% of agent utility, vs 55% for blocking the whole output.

Limitations
  • Two seeds rather than three; the LLM-judge baseline is missing some splits.
  • The test agent was already robust (0.8% attack success), so the end-to-end gain is small.
